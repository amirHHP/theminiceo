**Dual-Track Agile** is a way of working that Jeff Patton, and later SVPG, emphasized: a product team runs two tracks in parallel. **Discovery** validates the problem and the solution. **Delivery** builds what discovery has already shown is valuable and feasible.

## Why one track is not enough

Delivery-only teams fill the backlog with stakeholder requests and learn after release. Discovery-only teams produce reports and the product stalls. Two tracks mean that while the team is building this sprint, next sprint’s hypotheses are already being tested.

## How to run it

- One cross-functional team (product, design, engineering) owns both tracks. Do not create two teams that throw work over a wall.
- Discovery’s output is not a ready-made story. It is a decision: build, do not build, or run the next test.
- Delivery input should come from discovery, not from an executive email. Pair Dual Track with [Continuous Discovery](/en/frameworks/continuous-discovery/) and an [Opportunity Solution Tree](/en/frameworks/opportunity-solution-tree/).

Dual Track does not replace Scrum or Kanban. It sits on top of them.
