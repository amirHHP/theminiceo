If the previous books were about “how to build a product,” **Escaping the Build Trap** by Melissa Perri is about **“why so many organizations, despite working hard, still ship products that do not matter.”**

Melissa Perri uses the term “build trap” for companies that measure success by **the number of features released**, not by **the value created for the customer and the business.**

Treat this comprehensive summary as a map for getting out of that trap:

---

### 1. What is the Build Trap?

The build trap happens when an organization mistakenly believes that “the more we build, the more value we have created.”

* **Output:** Number of features, lines of code, and tickets marked done in Jira.
* **Outcome:** The customer’s problem getting solved, revenue going up, or costs going down.

> **Key point:** Organizations stuck in the trap confuse “output” with “outcome.” They are a “feature factory.”

### 2. The Value Exchange Loop

Melissa Perri argues that a business is a loop:

1. The customer has a problem.
2. Your product solves that problem (value for the customer).
3. In return, the customer gives you money or data (value for the business).
If your product only has features but does not solve a problem, this loop breaks and you are in the build trap.

### 3. The Product Kata

This is one of the most practical parts of the book. A “kata” is a repeating practice for reaching a goal. The steps:

1. **What is the end goal?** (for example, raising conversion to 20%)
2. **Where are we now?** (conversion is currently 5%)
3. **What is the biggest current obstacle?** (users get confused at checkout)
4. **What experiment should we run?** (simplify the checkout page design)
5. **What did we learn?** (did conversion change?)

### 4. Product strategy is not a “list”!

Perri says product strategy is not a list of features the CEO has ordered. Strategy is a **decision-making framework**. She proposes a hierarchy:

* **Vision:** Where do we want to be? (ten years from now)
* **Strategic intents:** What large business challenges must we solve? (this year)
* **Product initiatives:** How does our product solve those challenges?
* **Options:** Features we test in order to reach the goals.

### 5. Management roles in modern organizations

The book explains what PMs at different levels should focus on:

* **Junior PMs:** Focus on tactics and optimizing features.
* **Product leaders (VP/CPO):** Focus on overall strategy, connecting teams, and removing organizational obstacles.
* **Note:** In organizations stuck in the trap, senior managers also get involved in button and color details, because they have no clear strategy.

---

### Why this book matters in 2026

In a world where AI can write code and produce features at the speed of light, the risk of falling into the “build trap” is higher than ever. If you cannot prove as a product manager that your features create **outcomes**, automated tools will take your place on **output**.

### Final takeaway in one sentence:

**“Instead of asking how fast we can build this, ask why we should build it at all, and how we will know it worked.”**
