Harold Lasswell, as a political scientist, points out in his own theory that **workplace politics** can be understood as unwritten rules: these rules determine when and how employees get promoted, or deserve a budget to do a project, and who should not receive these benefits.

Politics has become established as a negative word, but using it at work is unavoidable. In the view of many organizational-behavior scholars, political behaviors are not inherently negative; they can even be useful for members of an organization or for the organization itself. Just as Aristotle pointed out that part of human nature is as a political animal. Whether you take part in political affairs or avoid taking part in them, these affairs have a large effect on your life and activities. So it will be hard to be indifferent to them.

## **The nature of political behavior**

One of the most interesting facts about politics in organizations is its two-sided nature. This verbal or non-verbal contract — “if you help me, I will help you” — forms the foundation of a political power base until the parties become directly hostile to each other. Experts believe that political behaviors are acquired and at first may conflict with a person’s behavioral justifications. But when they are warned away from these tactics, they will use them.

## **Individual and organizational reasons for political behaviors**

Political behavior both comes from people’s own traits — for example someone in the organization who has a strong need for power, freedom of action, security, and administrative rank, and tries to resort to political behavior — and from organizational factors. When organizational resources are declining, or when existing resource patterns are changing, specific political behaviors are very likely to appear.

### **Individual level**

At the individual level, researchers have identified particular traits (such as needs and other individual factors) that are likely associated with political behaviors. Employees and members of the organization who are autocratic have a very high tendency to accept risk, or have an external locus of control. It has been observed that these people act politically in a way that pays less attention to the results of their actions on the organization. It has also been seen that if someone has a strong need for power, freedom of action, security, or administrative rank, they try hard to take a political approach.

### **Organizational level**

Naturally, political activity is a characteristic of organizations, because most organizations employ a large number of people who have different individual traits. But the type of political behavior present in the organization varies widely. Although we accept the fact that individual differences can play an important role on political stages, existing evidence more strongly confirms that some particular conditions and cultures cause certain political behaviors to appear and be reinforced.

More precisely, we should say that when the organization’s resources decline, or when existing resource patterns are changing, particular political behaviors are very likely to appear. In addition, cultures whose distinctive features are a lack of trust, role ambiguity, an unclear performance-evaluation system, and popular decision-making provide opportunities for people to display political behaviors. When organizations reduce some of their activities to improve efficiency (so-called tightening up), they are also forced to reduce their resources. People, afraid of losing resources, take political actions so that whatever they have is not taken from them. But any kind of change, especially changes that require reallocating resources inside the organization, likely creates and increases conflict and therefore intensifies political activities. In an organization, the less trust there is, the more intense political behavior will become, and people will more often take illegitimate actions. So a high level of trust outweighs political behavior and prevents illegitimate actions.

## What is the difference between bad workplace politics and good workplace politics?

Bad politics at work is easy to recognize. These politics include using business consulting, discussion, maneuvering, innuendo, and rumor-spreading that people use to advance their own affairs. Bad and inappropriate politics are inherently about promoting one person’s position in any necessary and suitable way, and highly inappropriate politics are inefficient methods that may even depend on political or unethical beliefs and intentionally harm employees’ income from another person.

Good politics at work include increasing a person’s interests while considering other people’s rights or the desirable legal cases of that same organization. Good politics include acceptable methods for recognizing your collaboration, taking ideas seriously, influencing other people’s thoughts, and using business advisors, and what decisions are made in this domain. These politics may also include rumors about narcissistic, lazy, or untrustworthy colleagues that destroy appropriate methods. This approach also follows a higher goal and provides the ground for progress in domains related to your interests. Appropriate phrases for doing appropriate politics include being understanding and having mutual understanding, proper networking, and gaining the skill needed for living in large cities related to people’s presence in society and managing officials. ### Social astuteness

### Interpersonal influence

Interpersonal influence is an ability to persuade people in order to influence how other people think. At first this method includes understanding people and their priorities and the related discussions, and then presenting your request to examine reactions.

### Networking ability

Networking ability forms the capacity and potential to create useful connections with a wide range of different people. ## **The domain of political activities**

People in organizations usually use political behaviors in 4 domains.

### **Structural change in the organization:**

Changing the organization’s structure comes with a reallocation of authorities; so changing responsibilities and duties affects the power base, and the result is a change in political behaviors to reach new power bases.

### **Coordination between organizational departments:**

The relationship between the organization’s main departments is not specified precisely. At times when closer relationships are created for joint decision-making among them, these relationships usually take on a political color.

### **Replacing a manager:**

To make this change you have to promote or transfer people and hire a new manager; so political behaviors form among employees at high levels.

### **Allocating resources:**

Given the importance of resources such as salaries, operating budgets, benefits, and so on, allocating them comes with political behaviors.

## **Some political techniques and methods**

Political tactics in the organization are those acts that people at every organizational level use to reach their goals. (Regardless of whether these methods are appropriate or not.) We name a number of these tactics here:

### **Attacking others or blaming them**

Disagreement and criticism at work is a natural issue, and not everyone thinks and views the same way, but among the different personalities you encounter at work, some people create conflict for their personal goals. Sometimes their goal is personal profit and benefit, and sometimes their goal is attracting attention and power, although they do not exist in every organization.

### **Using information as a political tool**

Holding back or deliberately distorting information is among them.

### **Hiding an undesirable situation and creating a desirable image of yourself**

This politics is in a way managing how others picture us. There are many cases for carrying this out. For example having a smile on your face even when you are upset, or choosing your type of clothing and grooming according to other people’s taste, and so on.

### **Creating a support base**

Gaining prior support for a decision, committing others to a decision by involving them in decision-making

### **Praising others (giving attention and favor to others)**

When we encourage and praise someone and praise them for having a trait or doing something, we have in fact satisfied their inner feeling. This act creates attraction and affection and also motivation or reinforcement for them, but praising people does not mean making empty compliments. Telling an employee they are wearing a beautiful shirt is a compliment. But when your employee handles an angry customer’s complaint and sends them away satisfied and happy, if you praise them you have made a valuable investment.

Of course in some people’s view, praising others in a group, or doing work that influential people like, is a kind of flattery.

### **A power coalition with strong allies**

The main type of collaborative strategy is a strategic alliance. A strategic alliance is a partnership of two or more companies or business units that jointly pursue important strategic goals, and all of them benefit from this partnership. In today’s business world, alliances among companies or business units have become common and customary.

Forming a team with strong people who can get work to a result, associating with influential people or even appearing connected to influential people, and creating a support network inside and outside the organization are all politics that can be used in organizations.
